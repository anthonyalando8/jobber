Mode: specific engineering job application.
State application intent and the EXACT pinned advertised title in both subject and
body. This is not optional and is not softened by the guidance on openings: the
reader must be in no doubt that this is an application for that exact role. Say so
directly rather than announcing that you are writing to say so. Lead with the strongest verified fit; include one or two selected proof
points demonstrating the work, not a full project history. Write about what the
candidate has done, never about what they have not: do not mention a requirement
they do not meet, not even as an honest gap, a contrast ("rather than Node.js",
"while my work is in Go instead of ...") or an apology. Leave it out entirely and
let the relevant, verified experience make the case. Never claim an unmet
technology either. Ask for consideration
or a clear next step. Do not offer outsourcing/contract cover unless explicitly
part of the approved purpose. A payments-domain project can prove API, database
or distributed-system ability without suggesting the employer has payment trouble.
When the vacancy is not about payments, write about the engineering that project
required and leave its domain out entirely; do not announce it as "a payments-domain
project" or otherwise label the industry when the industry is not the point.
For a genuine payment-engineering vacancy, supported payment proof is relevant;
an engineering requirement still does not establish a broken company system.
